You Lost Forty Thousand Pounds By Being Thirty Minutes Late

Prepared for Justin Dring · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
Why This Page Replaced The One We Had Ready
You were right on Friday and we had prepared the wrong thing

You said: “you'll have read your notes and prepped and thought about what's applicable to an independent solar consultant before we start just spitballing and asking questions.” Fair.

We had an audit ready about your website, your reviews and your enquiry form. All of it is still true and none of it is the thing costing you money this week. In four minutes you told us what is:

  • Fourteen quotes that had to go out before close of play
  • Staff standing waiting while you were on the phone, at £35–65 an hour
  • A £40,000 job lost last week because a quote was thirty minutes late
  • Dangerous situations on site needing reports closed off the same day

That is not a lead problem. You have fourteen sitting on your desk right now. It is a throughput problem, and you are the bottleneck in it.

The Forty Thousand Is The Only Number That Matters
And it is almost certainly not a one-off

A job is lost by thirty minutes once and it looks like bad luck. But the conditions that produced it have not changed — fourteen quotes in a queue, one person writing them, and site emergencies that legitimately jump the queue.

So the question is not whether it happens again. It is how often it has already happened without anyone counting. A job lost to lateness does not announce itself. The client simply goes with whoever answered first, and you never find out why.

⚠️ You will know of the forty grand because it was big enough to notice. The smaller ones do not get noticed, and there is currently nothing recording them.

What Could Come Off You, And What Could Not
⭐ Can be taken off you
  • Producing the quote document itself. Your numbers and specification, dropped into a template that formats and sends it. The thinking stays yours, the typing does not.
  • Chasing clients for information you need before you can price — automatically, not by you remembering.
  • Following up every quote that has gone quiet, on a timer, without you holding it in your head.
  • Scheduling and confirmations around site visits, including the reminders that stop no-shows.
  • Knowing what is outstanding — what is out, what it is worth, what has had no reply.
⛔ Cannot, and we are not going to pretend otherwise
  • The site visit. Somebody competent has to look at the roof.
  • The design and yield modelling. That is the product. Automating it would be selling you something worse than what you do now.
  • A dangerous-situation report. That is your name and your liability on a safety judgement.
  • The decision on a difficult job. Nothing replaces knowing when a site is not worth taking.

If the honest answer is that most of your time is in the second column, this is a hiring conversation and not a software one. We would tell you that rather than sell you a system that cannot help.

The Thirty Minutes, Specifically
Speed is a system property, not an effort property

You did not lose that job through carelessness. You lost it because fourteen things were ahead of it and nothing was telling you which one was about to go cold. Working harder does not fix that; the queue is the problem, not the effort.

What changes it:

  • The quotes sit in order of value and urgency, not in the order they arrived.
  • A quote that has been waiting too long surfaces itself instead of relying on you noticing.
  • The writing-up is minutes, not an hour, so a quote can go out the same day it was surveyed.
  • An automatic acknowledgement goes out the moment an enquiry lands — so the client knows they are with somebody even before you have priced it. ⭐ Often that alone is what beats the competitor who answered first.
And It Connects To The Other Thing You Said
Cancellations and quote speed are the same problem

You told Ruchi clients cancel late, sometimes once scaffolding is up. A client who waited a fortnight for a quote is a client who has had a fortnight to go cold, get another price, or change their mind.

Faster quotes do not just win more work. They win work from people who are still in the frame of mind they were in when they enquired — which is a different, more committed client than one who has been waiting.

The filtering described on the cancellations page and the speed described here pull in the same direction: fewer enquiries, handled faster, that actually proceed.

What We Would Want To Know
  • 1. How long from enquiry to quote, honestly? Not best case. Typical, in a week like the one you have just had.
  • 2. Of the fourteen, how many are repeat clients and how many are new? They need different handling and probably different speeds.
  • 3. What does a quote actually look like when it goes out? A document, an email, a spreadsheet? That decides whether this is a fortnight's work or an afternoon's.
  • 4. Who else could do the writing-up if the thinking were already done — Jack, or anybody?
  • 5. How many jobs do you think you have lost to lateness that you never found out about?
Five questions, and we would rather send them in advance than spend your time asking them live. That was the fair point you made on Friday.

Fourteen quotes, one person, and a forty grand job lost by half an hour.

More enquiries would have made that week worse, not better.
