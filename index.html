Finding 1 — Your Site Tells Google You Have 127 Reviews At 4.9. There Are None On The Page.
STRUCTURED DATA SERVED ON THE HOMEPAGE, 30 SEPTEMBER 2026 "@type" : "AggregateRating" "ratingValue" : "4.9" "reviewCount" : "127" "bestRating" : "5" review author : "Sarah Johnson" WHAT IS ACTUALLY VISIBLE ON THAT PAGE Star ratings shown to a visitor 0 Review text shown to a visitor 0 The word "review" in visible copy 2
This breaches Google's own structured data policy, whatever the truth of the numbers

Google's rules are explicit that review markup must be for reviews visible to users on that page. Marking up ratings that a visitor cannot see is against their guidelines regardless of whether those ratings are genuine.

The exposure is a manual action or loss of rich results, and you are spending on Google Ads from the same domain, so it is not a corner of the business you want a policy problem in.

And there is a second question we cannot answer for you

We do not know whether 127 reviews at 4.9 is real. We have not seen your review records and we are not going to assume either way.

But the single named author in the markup is “Sarah Johnson”, and that is the sort of name that arrives in a website template as filler. If that is what it is, it is the same kind of leftover we find on a lot of sites — except that this one is a claim about your reputation.

Ruchi's note says you are very big on trust with clients. That is exactly why we led with this. If the numbers are real, publish the reviews and the markup becomes an asset. If they are not, it needs taking out today.

The upside if the reviews do exist

You would get star ratings showing in search results and potentially in your ads, which is one of the few things that lifts click-through without lifting spend.

Right now you are carrying all of the risk of the markup and none of the benefit, because the reviews are not on the page for Google to validate against.

Finding 2 — 116 Pages, Most Of Them Around 300 Words
PUBLISHED SITEMAP: 116 URLs WORD COUNT, PAGES SAMPLED /claims 442 /book-consultation 399 /services/commercial 364 /services 320 /design-assessment 319 /free-quote 309 /post-install-qa 306 /contact 278 / (homepage) 468 A large site made of small pages.
This is the shape search engines have spent three years penalising

A hundred and sixteen pages averaging a few hundred words is the signature of scaled content, and Google's helpful content work has been aimed squarely at that pattern since 2023.

The irony is that you are the one business where depth would be easy. You sell feasibility studies, yield modelling, design assessment and post-install QA. That is technical work with genuinely useful things to say, and the pages describing it are three hundred words long.

What we would do instead of adding more pages

Take the eight or ten pages that actually earn money and make them properly deep. Post-install QA at 306 words could be the most useful page on the UK internet for somebody who suspects their install was done badly.

That also feeds the thing you told Ruchi you want. A page that answers a technical question in detail attracts somebody with a technical problem, and that person is a different sort of enquiry from somebody who typed “free solar quote”.

Finding 3 — Your Cancellation Problem Is A Qualification Problem, And It Is Fixable At The Form
WHAT RUCHI RECORDED FROM YOUR CALL "some of his clients are very unreliable, they cancel last minute or even once scaffolding has been put up" "wants a lot of leads but good quality reliable ones" Currently around 3-5 clients a week WHAT THE SITE CURRENTLY ASKS AN ENQUIRER Forms on the homepage 1 Qualifying questions none found Any commitment step none found
Somebody who cancels after the scaffolding is up was never committed at the enquiry

That is not a bad-luck problem, it is a filtering problem, and the filter belongs at the front. By the time scaffolding is standing you have spent real money on somebody who was never going to proceed.

A form that asks nothing gets you everybody. More leads through that same form gets you more of exactly the enquiry you say is causing the trouble.

What actually filters, in order of how hard it bites
  • Questions only a serious person will answer. Property type, roof or ground, timescale, whether they have had a survey, whether they own the building. Three extra fields cuts casual enquiries sharply.
  • Making the next step cost something small. You already have a Book & Pay page. A paid consultation, even a token one, is the single most reliable filter there is.
  • Scoring the enquiry before you call it, so the ones worth your time surface first rather than being worked in the order they arrived.
  • A deposit or a signed scope before scaffolding is ordered. That is a process change rather than a website change, and it is the one that stops the expensive version of the problem.
Which is also the honest answer to your pricing question

Ruchi says you asked whether it would be price per lead or a percentage of clients. We would not quote you per lead. Paying per lead pays whoever supplies them to send more, which is the mechanism that produced your cancellation problem in the first place.

We are not putting numbers in this document — that is a conversation for Friday once we know which of the above you actually want.

Finding 4 — The Domain You Gave Us Is Not The Domain You Run
WHAT YOU GAVE RUCHI independentsolarconsultants.com WHAT IT RESOLVES TO https://independent-solar-consultants.co.uk (redirect) Canonical on the live site: https://independent-solar-consultants.co.uk
The redirect works, so this is tidiness rather than breakage

Both resolve and the canonical is set correctly to the .co.uk, which is the right call. Nothing is broken.

It is worth knowing which one is on your vans, your email signature and your ads, because the hyphenated version is harder to say on the phone and easier to mistype. If the .com is the one people are given verbally, that is the one worth keeping front of house.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Resolve the review markup this week. Either publish the reviews so the 4.9 is visible and valid, or remove the AggregateRating. Leaving it as it is carries risk on a domain you are spending ad money from.
  • Put real qualification into the enquiry route. This is the one that addresses the cancellations rather than the volume, and it is the opposite of buying more leads.
  • Deepen eight to ten pages rather than adding to the 116. Starting with post-install QA and claims, because those attract people with a specific problem and a reason to pay.
  • Decide what you want turning on and off, since Ruchi noted you want that control. Worth being concrete on Friday about what the on and off states actually are.
  • Leave the analytics alone. It is already better than what we would have proposed.

You told Ruchi trust is the thing. Your homepage claims 127 reviews and shows none of them.

That is the first thing we would fix, and it costs nothing.
