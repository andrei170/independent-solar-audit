Somebody Who Cancels Once The Scaffolding Is Up Was Never Committed At The Enquiry

Prepared for Justin Dring · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
What You Told Ruchi
In your words, on 23 September

“Some of his clients are very unreliable, they cancel last minute or even once scaffolding has been put up.”

“Wants a lot of leads but good quality reliable ones.”

Those two sentences are in tension, and the tension is the whole problem. More leads through the form you have now gets you more of exactly the enquiry that is causing the trouble. Volume and reliability are not the same dial — and right now nothing on your site is turning the second one.

First, What Does One Of Them Actually Cost You?

We have deliberately left this blank. You know these numbers and we do not, and inventing them to make a case look good is exactly what you should distrust in anybody who pitches you.

 When somebody cancels after scaffolding is upYour figure
AScaffolding hire, erection and strike—
BYour own time — survey, design, specification, admin—
CAnything ordered or allocated that cannot be returned—
DThe slot in the diary that somebody else could have had—
EHow many times has that happened in the last twelve months?—
D is the one people forget, and at 3–5 clients a week it is often the biggest line. A cancelled week is not a week you get back.

A × E, plus B × E, plus D × E. That is the number this page is about. If it is small, nothing below is worth doing and we will say so.
Why It Happens — And It Is Not Bad Luck
What your form currently asks an enquirer
FORMS ON THE HOMEPAGE 1 QUALIFYING QUESTIONS none found COMMITMENT STEP none found

A form that asks nothing gets you everybody. The person who is three months from buying and the person who is idly curious fill in exactly the same four fields and arrive in your inbox looking identical.

You then do the surveying, the design, the specification and the ordering before anything has tested whether they were ever going to proceed. By the time scaffolding is standing, you have spent real money finding out something a form could have told you for nothing.

⭐ The filter belongs at the front, not at the survey.

⭐ The last two questions do most of the work

“Just researching” is the answer you most want somebody to give you honestly, because it costs you nothing to hear it on a form and a great deal to discover it when scaffolding is standing. Make it an easy, respectable answer and people will tell you the truth.

The bill upload is the sharpest filter on the page. Almost nobody without real intent will go and find a bill. Anybody who does is close to buying. Leave it optional — the point is not the file, it is who bothers.

⚠️ And the honest trade-off, said before you ask

This will reduce the number of enquiries you get. That is the intended effect, not a side effect. If anybody later reports a lower cost per lead as a win, they have measured the wrong thing. The number that matters is how many surveys turn into installs that actually happen.

The Gate You Have Already Built And Are Not Using
You publish a Book & Pay page

It is in your sitemap. A paid consultation, even a token amount, is the single most reliable filter that exists — far stronger than any question on a form, because somebody who has paid for a slot turns up to it.

You have built the mechanism. It is simply not standing between an enquiry and your diary.

Worth deciding on the call: is the paid consultation the default route with a free option for commercial enquiries, or the other way round? Either works. What does not work is having it available and unused.

The Four Filters, In Order Of How Hard They Bite
  • 1. Questions only a serious person answers. Ownership, roof or ground, spend, timescale, prior survey. Three or four extra fields cuts casual enquiries sharply and costs you nothing to add.
  • 2. Make the next step cost something. The paid consultation. Strongest single filter available to you, and already built.
  • 3. Score the enquiry before you call it, so the strong ones surface first instead of being worked in the order they arrived.
  • 4. A deposit or a signed scope before scaffolding is ordered. ⭐ This is the one that directly answers the thing you described. Nobody who has signed and paid a deposit cancels once the scaffolding is up.
Four is the fix for the specific problem you raised. One to three are what stop you needing it so often.
Why You, Specifically, Can Hold This To Account
This is the part most firms cannot do

You already run Google Ads conversion tracking, GA4, Hotjar and Microsoft Clarity. Session recording included. Most businesses we audit have none of it, and most agencies do not run that much on their own sites.

So you can actually watch what happens when the form changes. Where people drop out, which question loses them, whether the ones who complete it behave differently. That is a real before-and-after rather than an argument about whether it felt better.

⭐ It also means you could hold a per-lead or percentage model to account if you wanted to — which is unusual, and worth saying given the questions you asked Ruchi. We would still not recommend either, and the reasons are on the pricing page.
