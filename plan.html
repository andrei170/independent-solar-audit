Plan Of Action · Independent Solar Consultants Ltd

You Do Not Need Measurement. You Need A Filter And One Thing Taken Off Your Site Today.

Prepared for Justin Dring · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
Why This Plan Looks Different To Most
The first three weeks of our usual plan do not apply to you

Almost every plan we write opens with installing analytics, conversion tracking and a tag manager. You already run Google Ads conversion tracking, GA4, Hotjar and Microsoft Clarity. Session recording included. Most agencies do not run that much on their own sites.

So we are skipping all of it. What you have instead is a risk sitting on your homepage, a form that filters nobody, and a hundred and sixteen pages that are each too short to be worth much. That is a better set of problems to have, and a cheaper one to fix.

🔴 This Week — The Review Markup
Before anything else, and it is not a marketing job

Your homepage serves structured data claiming 127 reviews at 4.9 stars, with a single named author, “Sarah Johnson”. There is not one review visible on that page.

Google's rules are explicit that review markup must correspond to reviews a visitor can see. As it stands that is a policy breach regardless of whether the numbers are genuine — and you are spending on Google Ads from the same domain, which is not where you want a policy problem.

If the reviews are realIf they are not
Publish them on the page. The markup then validates against something and becomes an asset — star ratings in search results and potentially in your ads, which lifts click-through without lifting spend.Take the markup out today. Not this month. Today. It is a claim about your reputation and you told Ruchi trust is the thing you care most about.

We do not know which it is and we are not going to assume. You do, and it is a half-hour job either way.

Weeks One And Two — The Filter

This is the part that addresses the thing you actually described — clients cancelling late, and once after the scaffolding was up. The detail and the form concept are on the cancellations page.

StepWhatWhy it bites
1Rebuild the enquiry form with qualifying questions — ownership, roof or ground, annual spend, prior survey, timescale, optional bill uploadA form that asks nothing gets you everybody. Three or four extra fields cut casual enquiries sharply and cost nothing to add.
2Put your Book & Pay page in front of the diary⭐ You have already built it. A paid consultation, even a token one, is the strongest filter that exists — somebody who has paid for a slot turns up to it.
3Score every enquiry one to four automatically from the form answersSo the strong ones surface first instead of being worked in the order they arrived.
4A deposit or signed scope before scaffolding is ordered⭐ The direct answer to the problem you raised. Nobody who has signed and paid a deposit cancels once the scaffolding is up.
5Add click-to-call properly — there is one tel: link on the homepageYour highest-intent enquiries are the ones who want to speak to somebody now.
⚠️ Expect your enquiry count to fall

That is the intended effect, not a side effect. If anybody later reports a lower cost per lead as a win, they have measured the wrong thing. The number that matters is how many surveys turn into installs that actually happen.

⭐ And you are one of the few people who can prove it either way, because Hotjar and Clarity will show you exactly where people drop out of the new form and whether the ones who finish behave differently.

Weeks Two To Four — Depth Instead Of Breadth
116 pages, most of them around 300 words

Your published sitemap carries 116 URLs. Sampled pages run between roughly 280 and 470 words. A large site made of small pages is the pattern Google's helpful content work has been aimed at since 2023.

The irony is that you are the one business where depth would be easy. You sell feasibility studies, yield modelling, design assessment and post-install QA. That is technical work with genuinely useful things to say, and the pages describing it are three hundred words long.

So: no new pages. Take the eight or ten that actually earn money and make them properly deep.

⭐ Your post-install QA page, at 306 words, could be the most useful page on the UK internet for somebody who suspects their install was done badly. That person has a technical problem, money, and a reason to act — which is a completely different enquiry from somebody who typed “free solar quote”.

That is the same outcome the form is chasing, approached from the other end: attract the right person as well as filtering the wrong one.

Month Two Onward — Only Once The Filter Works
  • Scale the Google Ads, properly measured. You are already running them with conversion tracking. Once the form filters and the scoring works, you can increase spend against cost per qualified enquiry rather than cost per enquiry.
  • Formalise the referrals. ⭐ You told Ruchi referrals are where your work comes from, and there is no system behind them. A written referral arrangement and an automatic review request after every job turns an accident into a channel. Referred clients also cancel less, which is the whole theme of this plan.
  • Deepen the commercial side. You publish a commercial services page at 364 words. Commercial enquiries are larger, more considered and structurally more reliable than residential ones.
  • Then, and only then, more traffic. Pouring volume into an unfiltered funnel is how you got here.
The Thing You Mentioned To Ruchi That We Have Not Priced
“Loads of stuff to do”

Ruchi noted there may be admin work worth taking off you. We have deliberately not built a proposal around that, because we do not know what it is yet and guessing would be presumptuous.

Worth five minutes on the call: what is eating your week that is not the actual consulting? If it is quoting, chasing, scheduling or follow-up, that is a system rather than a person, and it is a cheaper fix than hiring. If it is something else, we will say whether it is ours to do.

“Built So I Can Turn It On And Off”
You asked for this and it is reasonable at 3–5 clients a week
  • The ad spend is yours, in your account, billed to your card. Pause it yourself, today, without asking anybody.
  • The management side is month to month. No lock-in, no exit fee.
  • Everything built stays yours — pages, forms, scoring, tracking, ad account, all in your name. If we part company you keep it, working.

⚠️ The honest caveat: stopping and starting costs you performance. Platforms relearn on every restart and rankings decay when content stops. Pausing a week is fine; pausing two months means paying to climb back. A lower steadier budget usually beats a higher one that keeps being switched off.

What We Need From You
  • 1. A straight answer on the 127 reviews. Real or template filler. Nothing else starts until that is settled, and it changes what we do with it.
  • 2. The questions that actually predict a good client. You have done 3–5 a week for a while — you know what the reliable ones have in common better than any form designer.
  • 3. A decision on the paid consultation — default route, or offered alongside a free one.
  • 4. Logins for the site, Google Ads and the analytics stack.
  • 5. Five minutes on what is eating your week.

Take the markup down or publish the reviews. Then fix the form.

Everything else on this page can wait a fortnight. Those two cannot.
